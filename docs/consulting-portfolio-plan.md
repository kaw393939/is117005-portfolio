# Consulting portfolio plan

**Status:** proposed design, not an implemented consulting service. **Reviewed:** November 17, 2026.

This research site is the workshop. The proposed personal portfolio is the public-facing result. Keep research and planning concise and navigable here; use selected, substantiated evidence to market consulting on the eventual portfolio. This document does not change existing course deadlines or grading.

## 1. Product direction

**Goal:** Help a prospective client understand the problem Keith can help with, inspect relevant work, and begin a qualified conversation.

**Positioning proposal:** *Practical AI adoption, grounded in education and software engineering.* Keith’s strongest distinction is connecting teaching, real software delivery and human judgment, not claiming to automate every business problem.

**Primary audiences**

| Visitor | Decision they need to make | Evidence they need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Education or training leader | Can Keith help our people use AI thoughtfully? | Teaching approach, workshop example, learning outcomes and boundaries. |
| Organization or technical lead | Can he turn our AI idea into a useful, testable project? | Architecture, documentation, implementation examples and evaluation method. |
| Potential collaborator | Are our questions and ways of working compatible? | Research agenda, project maturity and a clear contact route. |

Students remain a secondary audience with a direct route to learning resources. Do not make them navigate a sales funnel to find the research.

## 3. Proposed service themes

These are **offer hypotheses for Keith to review**, not existing packages, prices or delivery commitments.

1. **AI learning and workshops:** help a team understand tools, practice on a relevant task and evaluate its results. Proposed output: workshop plan, exercises and an evaluation checklist.
2. **AI workflow and prototype planning:** turn an unclear idea into a bounded use case, evidence requirements, architecture and testable pilot. Proposed output: decision brief, prototype scope and acceptance criteria.
3. **Educational experience design:** improve course or training information architecture, assignments and assessment. Proposed output: content audit, redesigned sample and implementation plan.

Each service should explain who it helps, the problem, proposed deliverables, what is outside scope and the first step. No guaranteed business results, universal timeline or pricing until approved.

## 4. Information architecture

Use at most five primary navigation items: **Services · Work · Insights · About · Contact**.

| Page | Question answered | Essential content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Is this relevant to me? | Compact value proposition, three service summaries, selected evidence and one primary contact link. No oversized hero, carousel or autoplay. |
| Services | What could we do together? | Audience, problem, output, boundaries, process and relevant work for each offer. |
| Work | What has he actually done? | Case studies with role, context, artifact, observed result, limitations and source. |
| Insights | How does he think? | Curated research and useful teaching resources, dates and evidence links; not an unfiltered archive. |
| About | Why trust him? | Verified experience, approachable personal story, teaching/building approach and professional links. |
| Contact | How do we start? | Approved private contact route, what information is helpful, and a warning not to send sensitive data. |

Case-study detail pages sit below Work. Existing learning and research URLs remain reachable. A public GitHub issue is not the default destination for confidential consulting inquiries.

## 5. Agile hierarchy

**Theme → epic → story → task.** A theme expresses an outcome; an epic groups a capability; a story is a useful visitor action; tasks implement it. An epic may span screens, so do not force every epic to equal one page.

### Theme A: Establish fit and trust

**Epic E1: Understand the offer**

**S1 — Identify fit (P0).** As a prospective client, I want to identify whom Keith helps and how, so I can decide whether to explore further.
- Given the home page, when I scan its opening content, then I can find the audience, problem and proposed help without playing audio or following an animation.
- The opening description is no more than 60 words; each service summary is no more than 45 words.
- In a review with three representative readers, at least two can name an audience and one service after a 30-second scan. Record actual results; do not invent participants.
- **Tasks:** draft positioning; select three service themes; assemble opening layout; run comprehension review.

**S2 — Compare services (P0).** As a client, I want consistent service descriptions so I can choose a useful starting conversation.
- Each service has the same six fields: audience, problem, output, process, exclusions and related work.
- Unconfirmed pricing, availability and timing are absent; proposed offers remain labeled during planning.
- Each service links to one relevant evidence item and the contact route.
- **Tasks:** write service records; map evidence; review scope language; test links.

**Epic E2: Inspect credible work**

**S3 — Evaluate a case study (P0).** As a client, I want to see Keith’s contribution and evidence, so I can judge relevance.
- At least three case studies cover education, software/tooling and organizational work.
- Every case has context, Keith’s role, approach, artifact, result, status, limitations and a dated source.
- An educational demo or planned runtime is labeled as such; unsupported outcomes are omitted or explicitly unknown.
- **Tasks:** evidence audit; write case studies; verify artifact links; review every outcome claim.

**S4 — Understand Keith (P0).** As a visitor, I want an authentic short biography and verifiable credentials.
- About separates career history, current focus and future questions.
- Professional roles and dates are checked against authoritative sources before release.
- No home address, student records, private Life Tracker content or defunct business branding appears.
- **Tasks:** verify biography; select authorized portrait; review role wording and privacy.

### Theme B: Make useful contact

**Epic E3: Start a qualified conversation**

**S5 — Make an inquiry (P0).** As a prospective client, I want a clear private contact route without an account requirement.
- Given any service page, when I activate Contact, then I reach the same approved contact destination in one navigation step.
- The page asks for a short problem description, organization/context and desired outcome; no required confidential documents.
- If a form is chosen, invalid input gets accessible feedback, failed sends do not claim success, and a non-form fallback is available.
- No fabricated response-time promise, external scheduler or new data processor is introduced without Keith’s approval.
- **Tasks:** confirm contact destination; choose mail link or form; test success/failure; document privacy handling.

### Theme C: Share knowledge without overwhelming people

**Epic E4: Find relevant insight**

**S6 — Explore research (P1).** As a visitor, I want a small number of topic routes and concise summaries.
- Insights groups material into AI practice, education and evidence/research, with descriptive titles and dates.
- Each research claim distinguishes source findings from Keith’s interpretation; sample limitations remain visible.
- Career job records retain check dates and links; stale listings never display as verified current openings.
- **Tasks:** content inventory; choose initial entries; add summaries and cross-links; validate provenance.

**S7 — Maintain accurately (P0).** As Keith, I want to update shared content once and see what release contains it.
- Shared identity, services and project status have one canonical record rather than copied facts across components.
- Each change links its story/issue, acceptance checks and commit; a preview is reviewed before release.
- An editorial checklist covers facts, dates, permissions, link health and planned-versus-delivered status.
- **Tasks:** content schema; ownership notes; build checks; release checklist and rollback procedure.

## 6. Architecture and quality plan

**Default proposal:** retain the current static VitePress/Vue stack unless a documented requirement justifies migration. It already publishes the portfolio and supports concise content. A framework change is not a design outcome.

- **Content:** Markdown case studies and articles; typed shared records for services, identity and project status. Keep research evidence separate from presentation.
- **Components:** small navigation, service, case-study and contact components. Apply single responsibility and clear interfaces; use patterns only for a named problem, not a pattern-count requirement.
- **Data flow:** reviewed source → structured content → static build → preview → human approval → deployment. No client database or authentication in the initial release.
- **Testing efficiency:** fast content/schema/link checks first; unit tests only for actual logic; integration tests for content rendering; a small Playwright set for navigation, mobile menu and contact. Do not use expensive end-to-end tests for every paragraph.
- **Static checks:** TypeScript checks, ESLint, formatting and secret scanning in CI. Pin tool versions and document commands before implementation.

### Cross-cutting acceptance criteria

- Keyboard users can navigate and reach all controls with visible focus; headings and landmarks are logical. Audio has a transcript, never autoplays and is optional.
- At 320px, 768px and 1440px there is no page-level horizontal overflow; 200% zoom preserves content and controls. Meet WCAG 2.2 AA for implemented content; automated scans supplement manual checks.
- On agreed mobile Lighthouse settings, median of three runs targets Performance ≥90 and Accessibility ≥95; report settings and actual scores, not a compliance guarantee.
- Each indexable page has a unique title, description and canonical URL. Structured data contains only supported facts; sitemap, redirects and broken-link checks pass.
- Text is readable, paragraphs are short, headings describe the information, and core content works without animation. No pop-ups, fake scarcity or distracting decorative charts.
- No new analytics, cookies, external integrations or public contact details without an explicit decision. Use synthetic examples when demonstrating student work unless publication permission is confirmed.

## 7. Delivery and decisions

**Milestone 1 — Evidence and plan:** approve positioning and offer scope; audit claims; finish stories, architecture and contact decision. No implementation yet.

**Milestone 2 — Small portfolio release:** implement S1–S5 and S7; preview, accessibility/privacy review and verified release. Preserve existing research resources and URLs.

**Milestone 3 — Iterate:** add S6, test with representative readers, improve the weakest journey. Add translation or interactive demonstrations only when they serve a real audience need.

**Definition of done:** every P0 criterion has recorded pass/fail evidence; public claims have sources; no critical/high test failures remain; contact works; Keith approves final copy and service scope; deployment corresponds to the reviewed commit and has a tested rollback.

**Decisions still needed before launch:** preferred consulting audience; which proposed offers Keith wants to provide; approved private inquiry destination; permission for any client/student imagery or testimonials. These do not block drafting the plan.
