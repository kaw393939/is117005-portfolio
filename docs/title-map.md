# A map of emerging AI work

An AI career is not a single job category. This portfolio groups the NY/NJ research sample by **the work someone owns**: getting a system into a customer's workflow, keeping its infrastructure running, testing its behavior, or helping an organization use it effectively.

Some labels are emerging; others describe familiar work with new AI responsibilities. This research does not establish when a title was invented. It also does not treat a collection of postings as a growth estimate. The [dated job sample](/jobs) records individual opportunities; the [BLS research](/research) supplies separate, broader occupational context.

## Deployment and customer engineering

**Forward deployed engineer (FDE)** combines hands-on engineering with customer discovery and implementation. The important question is what gets deployed. An FDE position can involve AI, conventional software, infrastructure, or several of these.

In this sample, [Palantir's Forward Deployed AI Engineer](https://jobs.lever.co/palantir/636fc05c-d348-4a06-be51-597cb9e07488) explicitly connects customer work to generative-AI delivery. Read the responsibilities before treating every FDE opening as an AI opening. Look for model integration, retrieval, evaluations, deployment ownership, and the amount of client travel.

## Agentic automation and enterprise IT

Agent work extends beyond prompting. Someone must connect enterprise data and tools, preserve permissions, diagnose failures, and make workflows observable. Relevant labels include agent engineer, automation engineer, AI integration engineer, and data-platform FDE; employers may combine these responsibilities under one title.

[Indicium AI's Forward Deployed AI Engineer](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/indiciumai/jobs/4944657101) illustrates this overlap through agents, MCP interfaces, enterprise data, and access controls. An established IT or data-engineering role can therefore be part of the AI field even when its title sounds conventional.

## AI security and red teaming

Distinguish **protecting an AI system** from **working in security at an AI company**. AI-specific security work may examine model misuse, untrusted inputs, agent permissions, evaluation methods, and safeguards. General security roles can instead emphasize networks, identity, cloud environments, incident response, or corporate systems.

The sample includes [MUFG's Global Red Team AI Engineer, Analyst](https://mufgub.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/MUFG-Careers/job/Jersey-City-NJ/Global-Red-Team-AI-Engineer--Analyst_10080289-WD-1) in Jersey City. It is an example of an explicitly AI-focused security title. An ordinary security internship at an AI-platform company is an adjacent pathway; the employer's industry alone does not make it AI red teaming.

## AI platforms, infrastructure, and reliability

Models depend on ordinary systems work as well as specialized infrastructure. This family includes AI platforms, ML operations, inference services, GPU infrastructure, observability, and site reliability. A useful description explains whether the employee builds the platform, operates it, supports its users, or manages hardware capacity.

Linux, networking, deployment automation, monitoring, and incident diagnosis can connect established systems careers to AI workloads. The sample's [CoreWeave Software and AI Engineer](https://boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/coreweave/jobs/4719916006) also shows AI meeting internal IT. Check whether a role operates AI infrastructure or applies AI to enterprise systems; these are different assignments.

## Adoption and enablement

AI adoption creates work in training, workflow discovery, coordination, and change management. These jobs can suit people who combine technical understanding with communication and business analysis.

The sample includes [Avis Budget Group's AI Enablement internship](https://avisbudget.wd1.myworkdayjobs.com/ABG_Careers/job/379-Interpace-Pkwy-Parsippany-07054/Accelerate---AI-Enablement-Intern-Summer-2027_R0190563) in Parsippany. Enablement is a different direction from training models: investigate who the users are, how adoption is measured, and whether the role also implements workflows.

## Evaluation, assurance, and AI-lab interfaces

Evaluation work asks what a system does reliably and where it fails. Titles may emphasize evaluations, benchmarks, quality, assurance, data curation, or research engineering. Some jobs mix technical testing with customer-facing delivery.

[Anthropic's Applied AI Demo Engineer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5455961008) includes evaluation harnesses and fallback behavior alongside demonstrations. Evaluation is also a responsibility inside other engineering roles; it does not always appear as a separate job title. Look for ownership of test datasets, failure analysis, and measurable release criteria.

## Risk and governance

Governance work concerns inventories, risk assessments, review evidence, accountability, and ongoing monitoring. This can appear in dedicated AI-risk titles or within established audit, privacy, compliance, and model-risk functions. [BlackRock's AI Risk Oversight, Director](https://careers.blackrock.com/job/new-york/ai-risk-oversight-director/45831/102830380048) supplies a senior example. Technical depth varies: policy coordinators, model validators, and engineers can collaborate without sharing the same qualifications.

## Product, delivery, and operations

AI products also need scope decisions, rollout plans, adoption measures, and operational ownership. [OpenAI's Deployment Lead, FDE- NYC](https://openai.com/careers/deployment-lead-fde-nyc-new-york-city/) illustrates delivery leadership around engineering teams and customer outcomes. Product managers and operations specialists may own different parts of the same implementation.

## Compare requirements, not just labels

Internship, entry, mid, and senior classifications should follow eligibility and responsibilities. An internship can require prior experience or a particular graduation year. A role asking for one to five professional years is not a no-experience opening. When the employer gives no rank, this portfolio marks its classification as an inference.
