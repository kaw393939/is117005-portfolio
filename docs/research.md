# Where is the growth?

AI creates work across software, data, infrastructure, security, and organizational decision-making. The strongest research question is: **which tasks are expanding, and what evidence supports that claim?**

Research checked **November 5, 2026**. This portfolio combines U.S. occupational projections with international employer research. These sources measure different things and should be read separately.

## U.S. occupational outlook

The table uses one consistent Bureau of Labor Statistics projection period: **2025–2035**. Percentages are rounded as published in the Occupational Outlook Handbook, updated August 27, 2026.

| Broad occupation | Projected employment growth | Connection to AI | Source |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Data scientists | **35%** | Analyze data and develop predictive methods | [BLS data scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) |
| Computer and information research scientists | **22%** | Investigate computing methods, including AI | [BLS research scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/computer-and-information-research-scientists.htm) |
| Information security analysts | **21%** | Protect systems as organizations adopt technologies such as AI | [BLS security analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm) |
| Software developers | **10%** | Build software; AI applications are one demand driver | [BLS software developers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) |

For comparison, BLS projects **3%** growth across all occupations over the same period. The developer figure refers to software developers specifically; it does not refer only to AI developers. Security analyst growth is not an AI security engineer forecast.

Growth measures the change in employment over a decade. Annual openings also include replacement hiring and are a different measure. Neither tells us how many beginner jobs are available today.

## International employer expectations

The World Economic Forum's *Future of Jobs Report 2025* places AI and machine learning specialists among the fastest-growing roles expected through 2030. Its survey covers more than 1,000 employers representing over 14 million workers across 55 economies. These are employer expectations, not a U.S. occupational forecast. [WEF report](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/) · [Jobs outlook chapter](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/2-jobs-outlook)

## Signals for newer titles

The AI Workforce Consortium's September 2025 report studies 50 selected roles across G7 countries, using July 2024–June 2025 data and the preceding year for comparison. It identifies AI/ML engineers and AI risk and governance specialists among rapidly growing demand areas. That is a historical signal within a selected dataset, not a forecast for every AI title. [Report, methodology p. 19 and demand table p. 24](https://www.cisco.com/content/dam/cisco-cdc/site/m/ai-workforce-consortium/documents/2025-ai-workforce-consortium-full-report.pdf)

## What this means for a student

Treat a title as a starting point for investigation. Compare duties across several postings, check location and experience requirements, and build evidence of a useful skill. A growing field can still have a competitive entry market.

This site's emerging-title cards show **no numeric forecast**. MLOps, AI product management, governance, and security have useful responsibilities to explore, but this research does not establish that every title is growing at a measured rate.
